# Moonshots

**Why.** The underlying flow is getting sorted: sandboxed execution, honest
review, instant merges, run supervision. What sits ON TOP of it is still a
kanban board and a pile of scripts — interfaces designed for tracking work,
not for amplifying the human. This bucket is for first-principles redesigns
of how humans and the factory meet: greatly increase the human's ability to
brainstorm, visualize, inspect, and redirect rework — make the system a
perfect harness and a cost-efficient factory, not a task tracker with
agents attached.

**Where it stands.** Nothing built. The board UI shows tasks; everything
interesting (traces, costs, diffs, decisions, history) needs an expert with
a terminal.

**Ideas** (graded honestly against where the kit stands).
- The wave story: one page that tells a wave as a narrative — what was
  tried, what failed, what it cost, what changed — generated from the
  ledger, readable in two minutes. (Feasible now; Watching bucket's story
  page is the seed.)
- Brainstorm surface: the human sketches a half-formed idea; the kit
  instantly surrounds it with twins from history, cost quotes, drastic
  alternatives from scout waves, and a "what would have to be true" list —
  thinking WITH the human, not queueing behind them. (Needs Memory bucket
  landed first.)
- Live factory floor: a single visual where specs, runs, merges, costs, and
  budgets move in real time — inspect any piece by clicking into its
  evidence. Mission control, not a kanban. (Feasible after 209/210 ledgers.)
- Rework by conversation: point at any merged change and say what's wrong
  in one sentence; the kit builds the task, twins it, quotes it, and runs
  it — the human never writes a brief. (Needs twins + quote + merge flow.)
- Interfaces that fit the moment: the same system renders as a changelog
  for the returning human, a war room during an incident, a portfolio view
  across projects — one ledger, many first-principles lenses.

When an idea here becomes real work, it gets its own section below this
line (or its own file) with the design, the tasks, and the before and after
numbers. The WHY above travels into every task description.
